> **💼 Career Launchpad** is the **reference/dogfooding agent**. Its persona pair **(Jordan + Rachel)** serves as
> the active validation case. The other two persona pairs (Alex/Maya, Priya/Dr. Kenji) are **archived for reference**.

## Pair 1: 🚀 The Startup Thinker (⚠️ ARCHIVED)

### Consumer: Alex Chen

| Attribute      | Detail                                                                                          |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Role**       | Aspiring or early-stage founder (idea / pre-seed stage)                                         |
| **Age**        | 22–35                                                                                           |
| **Background** | Student with an idea, professional leaving a job to build something, or first-time entrepreneur |
| **Income**     | Low to moderate — bootstrapping or living off savings                                           |

**Core Problem:** _"I have a startup idea — will people actually want it?"_

**Triggers:**

- Just had a "great idea" and can't stop thinking about it
- Friends/family question the idea — can't confidently defend it
- Drowning in contradictory advice — "talk to users," "build an MVP," "validate first"
- Afraid of wasting months building something nobody wants

**Current Behavior:**

- Reads startup blogs (YC, Lenny's Podcast, Indie Hackers)
- Posts on r/startups, r/entrepreneur asking for feedback
- Tries ChatGPT/Claude for generic validation advice
- Talks to friends/family (biased, polite feedback)

**Why Existing Tools Fail:**
| Tool | Why It Falls Short |
|------|-------------------|
| ChatGPT/Claude | Generic advice — doesn't know Alex's specific idea |
| Lean Canvas | Static template, no ongoing guidance, no challenge engine |
| YC Startup School | Passive learning — doesn't actively challenge assumptions |
| Reddit/forums | Unstructured, conflicting opinions |

**Frustrations:**

- Vague advice without a step-by-step process
- Confirmation bias — unconsciously validates own ideas
- Analysis paralysis — too many frameworks, not enough action
- No one to ask hard questions and force deeper thinking

**Desired Outcome:** Alex wants to know with confidence: Does this problem exist? Who has it? Will they pay? What should I do NEXT?

**Success Signal:** A clear, actionable validation plan — not just an idea.

**Evidence vs. Assumptions:**
| Label | Item |
|-------|------|
| **EVIDENCE** | Founders struggle with idea validation and often build before verifying demand |
| **EVIDENCE** | Confirmation bias is a known cognitive trap in startup validation |
| **ASSUMPTION** | Would pay for or actively use a specialized AI validation agent |
| **ASSUMPTION** | Prefers structured guidance over open-ended chat |

## Pair 2: 🌍 Study Abroad Navigator (⚠️ ARCHIVED)

### Consumer: Priya Sharma

| Attribute      | Detail                                                                            |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Role**       | Student (high school senior, undergrad, or recent grad)                           |
| **Age**        | 17–26                                                                             |
| **Background** | International student seeking to study abroad (US, UK, Canada, Australia, Europe) |
| **Income**     | Low to middle — financial constraints are a major factor                          |
| **Technical**  | Comfortable with apps, web research, social media                                 |

**Core Problem:** _"I want to study abroad — where do I even start?"_

**Triggers:**

- Realizes home country education won't help reach career goals
- Parents want her to go abroad but don't understand the process
- Friends are applying abroad — feels left behind
- Approaching graduation — "what next?" panic

**Current Behavior:**

- Researches universities on Google, QS rankings, Times Higher Education
- Browses Reddit (r/studyAbroad, r/IntlStudents), The Student Room
- Creates messy spreadsheets tracking deadlines and requirements
- Uses scholarship search sites (Fastweb, Scholarships.com) — overwhelming
- Watches YouTube videos from current international students

**Frustrations:**

- Information overload — too many options, no personalization
- Fear of missing critical deadlines
- Cost uncertainty — tuition, living costs, currency confusion
- Visa anxiety — complex, high-stakes processes
- Feeling alone — friends/family haven't done it

**Desired Outcome:** A single source of truth matching her profile to countries, universities, and scholarships — with clear deadlines, checklists, and a step-by-step timeline.

**Success Signal:** A personalized study abroad plan with target universities, scholarship strategy, and monthly action checklist.

**Evidence vs. Assumptions:**
| Label | Item |
|-------|------|
| **EVIDENCE** | 18-24 month fragmented process with no integrated tool |
| **EVIDENCE** | Financial toxicity is #1 barrier for international students |
| **ASSUMPTION** | Students would trust an AI agent for this high-stakes process |
| **UNKNOWN** | Willingness to pay vs. free alternatives and paid consultants ($500–$5,000) |

## Pair 3: 💼 The Career Launchpad (✅ ACTIVE)

### Consumer: Jordan Williams

| Attribute      | Detail                                                     |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Role**       | Final-year student or recent graduate (any discipline)     |
| **Age**        | 20–28                                                      |
| **Background** | Any major — engineering, business, arts, sciences          |
| **Income**     | Low to none — supported by family or working part-time     |
| **Technical**  | Comfortable with LinkedIn, job portals, productivity tools |

**Core Problem:** _"I want my dream career — am I actually ready?"_

**Triggers:**

- Graduation approaching — "real world" is coming
- Rejection fatigue — dozens of applications, few responses, lots of ghosting
- Peer comparison — friends are getting jobs
- Skills anxiety — job descriptions require skills they don't have
- Direction uncertainty — not sure what career path is right

**Current Behavior:**

- Scans LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor
- Spray-and-pray applications
- Asks ChatGPT for generic resume tips
- Watches "how to get a job" YouTube videos
- Occasionally visits underfunded university career center

**Frustrations:**

- Ghosting by recruiters
- Unclear expectations — impossible-sounding job requirements
- No honest feedback on why they're rejected
- Skills gap confusion — "should I learn Python? Figma? SQL?"
- Portfolio anxiety — don't know if their work is good enough
- Career direction confusion — afraid of picking the wrong path

**Desired Outcome:** Know what career paths fit, what skills are missing, whether their resume/portfolio is competitive, and have a concrete plan.

**Success Signal:** Personalized career roadmap with skill gap analysis, improved resume, target job list, and interview prep plan.

**Evidence vs. Assumptions:**
| Label | Item |
|-------|------|
| **EVIDENCE** | Entry-level job market is toughest in 5 years |
| **EVIDENCE** | Skills gap between education and employer needs is well-documented |
| **EVIDENCE** | Ghosting and rejection fatigue are major sources of graduate anxiety |
| **ASSUMPTION** | Would be honest with an AI about skill gaps and insecurities |
| **UNKNOWN** | Whether free tools (LinkedIn, YouTube) are "good enough" for most |
