# Building your persona and training a skill — what to expect

Written from two simulated creators taken through the real product (see the case-study folders). Numbers are from those runs.

## What you bring (15 minutes of prep)

The Architect interviews you; it does not need documents. It goes faster if you have answers to these in your head, in your own words:

| It will ask | Example (Priya, career coach) |
|---|---|
| Who you help, and who you send elsewhere | Mid-career PMs, 4–12 years. Not fresh grads; I refer them to a cohort. |
| The two or three things you always ask first | "What's different in 12 months if this works?" "What have you tried?" |
| Your rules, each with the reason | "Referrals over portals, because 70% of my clients' offers came through intros." |
| How you talk | Short sentences, blunt but warm, no exclamation marks, "Okay, here's what I'd do." |
| What you hand off, and the sentence you use | Visas → immigration consultant. Tax on ESOPs → a CA. Mental health → a therapist. |
| One real case, start to finish | Rahul: 30 portal applications, zero calls → story in 2 weeks, 22 intro messages, 2 offers in 9 weeks. |

The reasons behind your rules are the asset. A rule without a reason reads like any coach.

## What happens

1. **Persona (about 6 exchanges).** The Architect asks the questions above, drafts your persona in the first person, reads it back in three lines, and asks for one correction. It sets your name and tagline and makes the persona public.
2. **One skill (about 8 exchanges).** It names the skill as a verb plus an object (`coach-pm-transition`, `review-system-design-doc`), interviews for the playbook, writes it in the platform template, and writes one file per recurring topic with an index.
3. **Real test.** It runs your persona on your real case's opening message and shows you the actual reply. You say "I'd have said that" or give one correction.
4. **Publish.** The skill gets a title, a one-line hook, and a category, and goes live on Explore attached to your persona.

Time: 20–45 minutes of your attention. Each Architect turn takes 30–90 seconds because it is reading its playbooks and running your persona for real.

## What comes out

- A persona prompt: who you help, who you don't, what you ask first, your rules with reasons, how you talk, your hand-offs.
- A skill playbook with seven fixed sections: intake, rules, process with phases and done-when, topic index, output format, boundaries, worked example.
- Topic files (3–8) with how you go about each recurring situation.

## What "good" looked like in testing

Simulated clients talked to the personas and a strict judge graded each conversation on seven criteria, 0–3 each (21 max), against the creator's own profile.

| Creator | Round 1 | After one revision |
|---|---|---|
| Priya Nair (coach) | 19 / 16 / 17 | 20 / 20 / 15* |
| Dev Raghavan (review) | 19 / 19 | 21 / 20 |

\* The third client was outside Priya's scope (a fresh grad); how much to help someone you refer out is a creator decision, and both extremes are in the case study.

What clients said afterwards: "I finally have a tight story, an outcome-first resume, and a concrete 5-DMs-a-day plan to start tomorrow." "The idempotency deep dive was the exact fix I needed."

## What went wrong and got fixed (so you don't hit it)

- The persona promised to "send the cohort intro and cc you". It cannot send anything. Now every persona says "I can only talk and write here" and drafts instead.
- A hand-off the creator mentioned (tax → CA) never made it into the prompt. Now every hand-off you name is checked against the prompt before it's written.
- The persona gave a plan before asking the always-first questions. Now intake finishes before any prescribing.
- A stressed student got a seven-day plan across three files. Now volume matches the person: one next step for someone anxious or new.
- The intake section absorbed the worked example's details and became a ten-field form. Now intake stays generic to the skill and conversational.

## Two ways a revision can break your persona (and what now stops them)

- **Dictating an exact format.** Priya asked for a first reply that "must be exactly two lines, nothing else". The persona obeyed so literally that it dropped her hand-offs and started giving tax advice; scores fell from 20 / 18 / 20 to 13 / 7 / 5. The Architect now refuses rigid format rules, tells you why, and proposes the behavior instead ("even with a deadline, I open with my two questions, one line each, then the offer intake"). Every persona also keeps its last ten prompt versions, and the Architect can roll back.
- **A quiet model switch.** The same revision moved her persona from the provider's default model to a smaller one. Replies got fast and generic. The Architect no longer changes a persona's model unless you name one.

## Your part after launch

Watch a few conversations. Come back to the Architect with what you'd have said differently, the way Priya did: "it should have sent them to a CA", "one lever at a time". Those revisions took one short session each and moved the scores.
