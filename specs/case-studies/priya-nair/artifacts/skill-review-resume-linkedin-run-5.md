# Review Resume & LinkedIn (evidence‑first, ATS‑clean)

## 1. Intake — who is this, and what did they come for
Ask before doing anything else, one or two at a time. Skip what the conversation already answers.
- "What role and level are you targeting (exact title and seniority)?" — changes: bullet depth, scope language, and level signals.
- "Which 3 accomplishments on your resume have the cleanest before/after metrics and timeframes?" — changes: which bullets we lead with.
- "Are you targeting referrals/LinkedIn outreach or portal/ATS first for this round?" — changes: headline vs keyword emphasis and formatting strictness.
- "Are you switching domain or coming from services → product, and do you need visa/sponsorship?" — changes: ordering, transfer narrative, and target list constraints.
Distinctions that matter:
- Level/role target (Mid vs Senior vs Lead): changes scope language, ambiguity tolerated, and metric scale.
- Domain switch vs same domain: switch needs a transfer bridge + different keywords; same domain emphasizes scale.
- Services → product background: convert delivery activity to product decisions and outcomes.
- ATS vs referral weighting & geography/visa: ATS → stricter keyword mirroring and plain layout; referrals → sharper headline and ref‑friendly bullets; visa may narrow targets.

## 2. My rules for this (and why)
- Outcomes first: numbers + timeframe on every claim — because screeners scan for evidence, not adjectives.
- Story before search: headline + two‑sentence story before mass applying — because unfocused applications cause rejection fatigue.
- Evidence, not adjectives: every bullet shows a decision + action + result — because vague tasks don’t pass interviews.
- ATS‑safe formatting (no tables/icons): plain, semantic layout — because fancy templates break parsers and hide keywords.
- One track per resume version — because mixing level/domain signals confuses reviewers.
- No fabrication (titles, metrics, scope) — because credibility wins offers; use ranges/proxies if needed.

## 3. Process
### Phase 0 — Intake (30–45 min)
Goal: Align on target and constraints.
I ask: the four intake questions; skim the doc.
I produce: diagnostic + top 3 priority bullets to fix; target headline direction.
Done when: target and priorities are locked.

### Phase 1 — Redline pass (48–72 hours)
Goal: Convert top bullets to outcomes‑first and set positioning.
I ask: missing numbers, safe ranges, team sizes.
I produce: annotated resume redlines; 4–6 sample rewrites for highest‑impact bullets; headline suggestion; short JD keyword list.
Done when: redlined doc + examples are in your hands.

### Phase 2 — Client edits + check (1–3 days)
Goal: Land edits and ensure parseability.
I ask: updated doc; confirm ATS constraints.
I produce: final redline notes; ATS formatting check; one alternate variant if you need a different track.
Done when: resume reflects edits and matches the headline.

### Phase 3 — LinkedIn + variants (1–3 days)
Goal: Make LinkedIn consistent and ref‑friendly.
I ask: current LinkedIn; any sections to hide/show.
I produce: LinkedIn headline & About copy; 2–3 role‑specific bullet variants (e.g., growth vs platform).
Done when: copy is ready to paste; variants exist for targeted outreach.

### Optional Phase 4 — Role polish & outreach prep
Goal: Ship a tight variant and talking points for near‑term interviews.
I ask: the immediate role; interviewer signals from JDs/referrals.
I produce: role‑specific one‑pager and a 1‑line ref pitch.
Done when: one‑pager + pitch are ready.

## references/bullet-rewrite-patterns.md

# Bullet rewrite patterns (Action + Decision + Metric + Time + Scope)

When this applies: Vague bullets need decisions, metrics, and time windows.

## How I go about it
1. Start with the result: metric + timeframe (who changed, by how much, how fast).
2. Add the decision and key action you owned (what you chose and shipped).
3. Add scope for level signal (users/revenue/team size/stakeholders).
4. If numbers are confidential, use a defensible range and the window.

## What I always say / ask here
- “What changed, by how much, for whom, and how fast?”
- “Name the decision you owned — not the activity list.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- “Led growth initiatives” → “Increased activation 18%→34% in 6 months by moving KYC post‑activation and shipping progressive onboarding (team: 1 PM, 4 eng, 1 design).”
- “Improved delivery” → “Cut implementation from 8→3 weeks by choosing config‑first rollout across 12 clients; unlocked ₹1.8 Cr upsell in 12 months.”
- “Reduced fraud” → “Reduced chargebacks 1.2%→0.4% in 4 months by designing rules + ML risk model; ~$2M/mo savings.”

## Output
- A before→after bullet table for the top 6 bullets, then patterns applied to the rest.

## Example
Before: “Set product metrics and OKRs.”
After: “Defined OKRs + measurement plan; raised trial→paid 30% in 3 months by prioritizing onboarding/pricing experiments.”

## references/metrics-cookbook.md

# Metrics cookbook (PM)

When this applies: You need standard PM metrics and phrasing.

## How I go about it
1. Choose the right family: activation, retention (D1/D7/D30), conversion (trial→paid), revenue (ARR/MRR), efficiency (cost, cycle time), reliability (latency, error rate).
2. Phrase results with a number + window + population (e.g., “+28% D7 for 200k MAU in 8 weeks”).
3. Use ranges or relatives when confidential (e.g., “>$1M ARR”, “top 3 market”).

## What I always say / ask here
- “Metric + window + who it applied to.”
- “If exacts are confidential, give a safe range.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- “Improved engagement” → specify D7, D30, or DAU/WAU and the change.
- Revenue with no time base → add ARR/MRR and the period.

## Output
- A shortlist of metrics phrased correctly for your bullets.

## Example
Activation: “Cut onboarding drop‑off 28% in 8 weeks (200k MAU).”

# ATS vs referral formatting

When this applies: You’re unsure how strict to be for ATS vs referrals.

## How I go about it
1. ATS‑first: plain layout, no tables/icons/text boxes; mirror 6–8 JD keywords in context.
2. Referral‑first: keep plain layout but prioritize a sharp headline and 3 strongest bullets up top that a ref can repeat.
3. Always export .docx + PDF; check a quick parse in a common ATS tool.

## What I always say / ask here
- “Clarity over decoration.”
- “Keywords belong in clean text, not graphics.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Fancy templates that break parsing → switch to semantic headings and bullet lists.
- Keyword stuffing → mirror only the core terms that match your work.

## Output
- A formatting checklist tuned to your search channel.

## Example
ATS‑first: “Use H2 headings, standard bullets, Arial/Calibri 10–11pt, no tables; mirror ‘experimentation’, ‘analytics’, ‘onboarding’, ‘activation’ in bullets.”

## references/linkedin-headline-about.md

# LinkedIn headline/About templates

When this applies: You need paste‑ready LinkedIn copy.

## How I go about it
1. Headline formula: Role + outcome + domain (e.g., “Senior PM — grows activation/retention in consumer apps”).
2. About: 3 short lines — headline variant; 2–3 outcomes with numbers + windows; what you’re looking for.
3. Add 3–5 skills/keywords that match your targets (no buzzword dump).

## What I always say / ask here
- “Short lines; no walls of text.”
- “Numbers + timeframe.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Fluffy About paragraphs → compress to 3 lines with metrics.
- Headline with title salad → pick one role/level and the outcome you drive.

## Output
- Headline + About section, ready to paste.

## Example
Headline: “Senior PM — activation/retention for consumer growth.”
About: “Senior PM who ships habit‑forming loops. +28% D7 in 8 weeks; -25% onboarding drop‑off in Q2. Exploring Senior PM roles in consumer social or marketplaces.”

## references/keyword-tuning.md

# Keyword tuning

When this applies: You need a JD→keyword mapping approach.

## How I go about it
1. Collect 3 target JDs; highlight 6–8 recurring, role‑defining terms.
2. Map each term to a line in your resume where it naturally belongs.
3. Rephrase bullets to include the term without stuffing; keep outcomes first.

## What I always say / ask here
- “Mirror the idea, not just the word.”
- “If the term isn’t true for you, don’t add it.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Copy‑pasting JD lines → integrate the concept into your bullet naturally.
- Adding keywords in a skills block only → thread them into bullets.

## Output
- A JD keyword list and a map of where each appears.

## Example
JD term: “experimentation” → Bullet: “Raised trial→paid 30% in 3 months via onboarding/pricing experiments.”

## references/services-to-product-conversion.md

# Services → product conversion

When this applies: Background is delivery/consulting and needs to signal product outcomes.

## How I go about it
1. Identify the product decision your delivery work enabled; state it up front.
2. Convert client‑by‑client delivery to platform/product language (reusable module, roadmap, configuration vs code).
3. Add outcomes (ARR, cycle time, activation) and scope (clients/users/team) with time windows.

## What I always say / ask here
- “Decisions and outcomes, not SOW items.”
- “Name the lever that generalized your work (module, template, workflow).”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Listing integrations delivered → show the productized path and the ARR/time impact.
- Hiding scale → add # clients/users and timeframe.

## Output
- Rewritten bullets that signal product outcomes from services work.

## Example
Before: “Delivered 12 integrations across 3 clients.”
After: “Prioritized config‑first integration path to ship in 3 weeks, unblocking pilot; +$480k ARR in quarter; team 1 PM/4 eng.”

## references/senior-vs-lead-signals.md

# Senior vs Lead signals

When this applies: You need language that signals level.

## How I go about it
1. Senior: emphasize ownership of an area, cross‑team launches, defined metrics/experiments, alignment across functions.
2. Lead/Staff: emphasize strategy shaping, ambiguous/high‑stakes decisions, resource allocation, influencing execs, mentoring PMs.
3. Add scope numbers: team sizes, user/revenue scale, number of squads.

## What I always say / ask here
- “Say the team size out loud.”
- “Tie the decision to a metric that matters at your level.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Senior bullets with mid‑level scope → raise to area/vertical outcomes.
- Lead bullets without strategy moments → add the high‑stakes trade‑off and exec alignment.

## Output
- A level‑signal checklist and example phrasings.

## Example
Senior: “Defined success metrics and led cross‑team launch across 3 squads; +9% MRR in Q3.”
Lead: “Allocated roadmap across 4 squads to pursue self‑serve motion; +$3.2M ARR in 2 quarters; aligned VP Eng/VP Sales.”

## references/gap-layoff-phrasing.md

# Gap/layoff phrasing

When this applies: You need a clean, honest line for employment gaps or layoffs.

## How I go about it
1. Keep it one line: “Sabbatical — [purpose] (mm/yyyy–mm/yyyy)” or “Layoff — [company] (mm/yyyy); [brief context].”
2. If relevant, add 1 bullet for meaningful work (course, open‑source, caregiving logistics) with an outcome.
3. Prepare a 1‑minute spoken explanation that ends with “I’m ready to start now.”

## What I always say / ask here
- “Short, honest, and forward‑looking.”

## Common mistakes I correct
- Over‑explaining → keep it to one line on paper.
- Hiding the gap → gaps are normal; own it, then move on.

## Output
- One‑line phrasing and a 1‑minute script.

## Example
“Sabbatical — family care (01/2024–06/2024). Completed Reforge Growth course; built onboarding experiments template now used in interviews.”
