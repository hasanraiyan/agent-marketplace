On ESOP taxes in India
I’m not a tax advisor — for ESOP taxation and filings, please consult a Chartered Accountant (CA). I can help you draft the email and the exact questions to ask.

High‑level mechanics (so you know what to ask for)
- At exercise: Perquisite tax as salary on (FMV at exercise − strike) × shares. Employer withholds TDS via payroll. FMV for unlisted shares is as per a Category I Merchant Banker valuation on/near the exercise date (Rule 3(8)); companies typically use a dated certificate within an allowed window.
- At sale: Capital gains on (sale price − FMV at exercise). For unlisted shares, holding ≤24 months → STCG taxed at your slab; >24 months → LTCG at 20% with indexation. Listed rules differ; most Series B are unlisted.
- Possible deferral: If the company is a DPIIT‑recognized eligible startup and they opt in, ESOP perquisite TDS can be deferred to the earliest of 48 months from end of the financial year of exercise, sale of shares, or you leaving the company. Many startups don’t qualify or don’t operate this — confirm in writing.
- Liquidity risk: A short post‑termination exercise window can force you to exercise (and pay perquisite tax) without any liquidity. Negotiating a longer window (5–10 years) reduces this risk; refresh policy and buyback/secondary history also matter.

Exact questions to ask the company (paste into your email if you want)

- Please share the latest Merchant Banker FMV certificate used for pricing (date, validity window) and the current board‑approved FMV.

- DPIIT‑eligible startup status and whether ESOP TDS/perquisite deferral is operated in practice.
