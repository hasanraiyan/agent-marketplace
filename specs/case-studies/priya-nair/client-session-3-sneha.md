[file presented: /workspace/outputs/esop_taxation_guidance.txt]
When contemplating leaving a company after 2 years and holding ESOPs, it's important to understand the tax implications to minimize liabilities. Here's a guidance outline:

1. **Understand the Vesting Schedule**: 
   - Check how much of your ESOP has vested in the time you have been with the company. Only vested options can be exercised. 

2. **Exercising Your Options**: 
   - If you decide to leave, assess whether you should exercise your vested options. Exercising means you buy the shares at the grant price.
   - Exercising ESOPs could lead to taxable events, so understand the exercise price and fair market value at the time of exercise.

3. **Taxation at Exercise**:
   - While exercising ESOPs, the difference between the fair market value (FMV) and the exercise price can be subject to tax, typically as perquisites (treated as salary income).

4. **Taxation on Sale of Shares**:
   - Upon selling the shares, any gain is subject to capital gains tax. 
   - Short-term capital gains tax applies if the shares are sold within 12 months of purchase. If sold after holding for more than 12 months, it comes under long-term capital gains tax.

5. **Requesting Information from the Company**:
   - Request for the Fair Market Value of your shares at the time of your departure to understand the potential impact.
   - Ask the company if they allow a cashless exercise or provide any spread between exercise and sale market to aid in tax liabilities.
   - Inquire if there are any upcoming liquidity events which might provide an opportunity to sell shares.

6. **Potential Strategies to Minimize Tax Liabilities**:
   - Explore if the company offers Net Exercise or Cashless Exercise options to minimize out-of-pocket costs and liabilities at exercise time.
   - Consider consulting with a tax advisor to plan exercises and eventual sales to maximize tax efficiency.

Seeking clarification from the company regarding these aspects can help in strategizing the exercise and sale of your ESOPs to minimize tax outlay.
