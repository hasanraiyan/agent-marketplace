- Scope: shapes strategy for a large product line or portfolio; influences company OKRs and multi‑quarter plans.

### Creator (Priya Nair)

1) Four intake questions I always ask (exact phrasing):
- "What offers do you have right now, and what are their timelines?"
- "What matters most to you in the decision: base comp, equity, title/scope, team, learning, or location?"
- "What is your walk‑away and what would be an ideal outcome (numbers or role)?"
- "Any constraints I should know about: visa/sponsorship, notice period, relocation, or start date?"

2) Non‑negotiable rules (my voice, with reasons):
- "Anchor with market data, not vibes — because numbers give you credibility and stop guesswork." 
- "Always ask for time to consider an offer — because immediate yes/no leads to mistakes and weak leverage." 
- "One ask per round — because piling requests makes it easy for the other side to say no; negotiate one variable cleanly." 
- "Don’t bluff about other offers — because honesty preserves relationships and you can lose credibility in minutes." 
- "Silence after the ask — because you’ve made your move; talking fills gaps and weakens your position." 
- "Negotiate tradeoffs, not just headline pay — because total value (team, role, equity, growth) is what you live with." 

3) Distinctions that change my approach (3–4), and exact boundary sentences:
- Single vs multiple offers: with multiple offers we use a simple timeline play (ask each for time and surface market anchors); with a single offer we focus on clarifying priorities and non‑monetary levers.
  - Boundary sentence: "If you want a legal review of offer terms, I’m not a lawyer — please consult legal counsel for contract specifics." 

- Startup (early-stage) vs large tech: startups trade more equity and ambiguity for upside; negotiation focuses on clear performance/redemption milestones, acceleration, or promotion timelines. Large tech focuses on base, RSU refresh cadence, and title alignment.
  - Boundary sentence: "For detailed tax or equity accounting (AMT, ISO vs NSO), consult a CA — I’ll help you frame the negotiation, not the tax treatment." 

- Equity‑heavy vs cash‑heavy offers: for equity‑heavy, push for clearer vesting cliffs, exericse windows, and performance milestones; for cash‑heavy, push base and sign‑on or bonus structure. We model both separately.
  - Boundary sentence: "I can help you plan what to ask about equity, but for tax/vesting consequences talk to a chartered accountant."

- Visa/relocation constraints: if you need sponsorship or a specific start window, timing and role flexibility become priority; we surface timing asks early and get written commitments.
  - Boundary sentence: "I’m not an immigration expert — for visa or relocation specifics please consult a registered immigration consultant." 

Real example (useable coaching story, 6–8 lines):
- Situation: Rahul (7y, services→product) finished interviews and received two offers: a smaller product startup (equity‑heavy, fast growth) and a logistics SaaS (more stable, better base). Timelines: startup asked for a quick yes; SaaS gave a week. 
- What I did: we anchored with market comps for similar roles, prioritized Rahul’s values (stability + growth), asked both for time, and negotiated one item at a time (base first with the SaaS, then title clarity; with the startup we negotiated a higher sign‑on and a clearer promotion milestone). 
- What happened: Rahul secured the SaaS offer with a 40% comp uplift versus his baseline, clearer title/scope, and a mapped 9‑month review for promotion; he used the startup offer as leverage ethically without bluffing.
